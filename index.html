Case Study 01

Double Points Campaign: Measuring Incremental Member Spend

Campaign Effectiveness Incrementality Commercial Lift
Observed Lift: Campaign vs Comparison Baseline
Sanitized uplift in spend/member/day and Average Order Value (AOV)
Business Question:

Does a Double Points incentive mechanic meaningfully stimulate incremental spending, or does it merely reward transactions that would have occurred organically?

Approach:

Evaluated transactional telemetry across campaign dates against unexposed baseline cohorts and prior comparison periods to isolate true behavioral lift.

Key Findings:
  • Observed a ~40% increase in spend per member per day during the promotional test window.
  • Generated an associated ~30% uplift in Average Order Value (AOV), which indicates stronger basket expansion.
Insight:

Double Points acts as an immediate spending multiplier. When combined with minimum spend thresholds, it motivates basket enlargement without permanent discount dilution.

Recommendation:

Deploy Double Points selectively during strategic off-peak trading windows or for slower-performing units where incremental margin exceeds the cost of future point redemptions.

Impact:

Delivered measurable commercial incrementality while maintaining positive campaign ROI over baseline comparison periods.

Case Study 02

Voucher Campaign Analysis: From Campaign Conversion to Commercial Return

Funnel Economics 5.8x Return Commercial Return
Campaign Conversion Funnel Progression (%)
Eligibility → Transaction → Completion → In-Store Redemption
Business Question:

How can we evaluate the commercial viability of injected voucher campaigns to ensure they drive genuine spending returns rather than unmeasured discount costs?

Approach:

Modeled the full end-to-end campaign funnel: Eligibility → Transaction → Completion → Redemption → Spend → Cost → Return, evaluating the spend-to-cost ratio across participant cohorts.

Key Findings:
  • Direct injected cash vouchers achieved a sustainable 5.8x spend-to-cost return among redeeming members.
  • Reactivated lapsed members (inactive for 90+ days) accounted for approximately 68% of total voucher-driven spending.
  • Cash vouchers encouraged greater basket stretch over item giveaways, which frequently compressed average check size.
Insight:

Campaigns should be evaluated on bottom-line commercial return, not just top-of-funnel redemption volumes. Targeting dormant cohorts yields superior incrementality compared to rewarding regular shoppers.

Recommendation:

Refine audience eligibility rules: prioritize voucher allocations toward dormant and at-risk members while enforcing minimum spend thresholds to safeguard unit economics.

Impact:

Protected campaign margins while delivering quantifiable revenue reactivation from high-value lapsed segments.

Case Study 03

Retention & Repeat Transaction Analysis: Understanding the Customer Lifecycle

Cohort Diagnostics Lifecycle Timing CRM Timing
Observed Cohort Progression (%)
Progression drop-off across sequential transaction milestones
Business Question:

Where do newly acquired members drop off along their transaction lifecycle, and what is the optimal timing window for CRM re-engagement?

Approach:

Tracked sequential transaction milestones across newly onboarded member cohorts and calculated the median elapsed days between successive visits.

Key Findings:
  • Transaction 1 → 2 conversion was ~45.23%, dropping to ~30.50% for 2 → 3, and ~8.87% for subsequent visits.
  • The median interval between 1st and 2nd transaction was ~44 days, but contracted to ~12 days between 2nd and 3rd visits once habituation occurred.
Insight:

The first-to-second transaction is the largest lifecycle drop-off, suggesting an opportunity for earlier CRM re-engagement before member interest diminishes.

Recommendation:

Deploy automated re-engagement messaging around Day 14–21 with time-sensitive incentives for unreturned members, accompanied by holdout control groups to measure true incremental return visits.

Impact:

Established a targeted lifecycle intervention window that compresses time-to-second-purchase and improves baseline cohort retention.
